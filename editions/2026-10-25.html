AI hits limits on money, chips and security

The Trends

  1. 12 showsBanks are locking AI assistants out of customer accountsWhat's the story
    • On The Chopping Block, venture capitalist Robert Leshner said banks restricted automated AI assistants from customer accounts, citing security. Blocking automatic transfers protects $1.6 trillion in deposits that pay savers nothing, because an assistant could sweep that cash into higher-paying accounts.
    • On The Compound, hosts Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson noted that JPMorgan Chase and Bank of America are blocking AI agents from consumer accounts. The point for AI optimists: big institutions can slow a disruption with simple security and regulatory walls that protect their fee income.
    • The hosts of The Chopping Block accused banks of bad faith, but the security worry has a real basis: AI agents that hold your login create fraud and legal-liability questions that consumer-protection laws never anticipated. Banks will likely lean on that argument if regulators push them to open up.
    What's next
    • Consumer banking regulators' stance on automated account access: if banks may keep blocking AI software, savers will have to move idle cash to higher-paying accounts by hand.
    • Rules on fraud and liability when an AI holds your login: clear rules would remove the banks' strongest argument for blocking assistants and open the door to AI that moves your money.
    • Whether any big bank offers a secure, approved way for AI assistants to move customer cash: if one does, the blocks look more about protecting deposit profits than preventing fraud.
    On: The Chopping Block  ·  The Compound
  2. 24 showsLenders and factory limits choke off the race to build AI data centersWhat's the story
    • On The Cognitive Revolution, SF Compute Chief Executive Evan Conrad said lenders now demand full cash upfront and three-to-five-year leases for AI chips because banks cannot value repossessed servers. Young AI startups without huge venture funding are locked out of computing power, leaving model building to cash-rich tech giants.
    • Financial hosts Michael Batnick and Ben Carlson noted on The Compound that bonds for AI cloud provider CoreWeave fell below 80 cents on the dollar, pushing its borrowing rates to 15%. Lenders are now pricing AI data centers like distressed debt, which could slow the borrowed money fueling chip stocks.
    • Even with unlimited cash, Intel chief technology officer Pushkar Ranade told Dwarkesh Podcast that chip factory output can only double over several years because toolmakers like ASML cannot build machines faster. The Cognitive Revolution added that memory chip supply can grow at most 2.5x by 2030, so AI services will likely stay scarce and expensive.
    What's next
    • CoreWeave's bond yields: if borrowing costs stay near 15%, debt markets are cutting off the AI data center buildout and chip orders will slow.
    • Trading volume on SF Compute's marketplace for pre-booking chip time: if buyers show up, banks will have a clear resale price for AI hardware and can lend to small startups again.
    • Customer schedules for Intel's next chip factories: with fixed costs at 80% of a plant, any client delay will punish Intel's finances against larger rival TSMC.
    On: Dwarkesh Podcast  ·  Latent Space  ·  The Cognitive Revolution  ·  The Compound
  3. 34 showsAI-powered hacking is exposing old software flaws faster than companies can fix themWhat's the story
    • On No Priors, web security firm Cloudflare chief technology officer Dane Knecht warned that major software flaws now surface weekly, forcing engineers to spend over half their time on defense for 18 months. New features in your everyday apps could slow while developers patch code before AI-armed attackers get in.
    • Dragonfly investor Haseeb Qureshi reported on Empire that AI models cracked a 2004 web software bug and pushed attack attempts on Cloudflare from hundreds per hour to millions. Separately, commercial chatbots from OpenAI and Anthropic refuse cyber testing prompts, so defenders are turning to free-to-modify models run on private servers.
    • CrowdStrike chief executive George Kurtz said on No Priors that automated hackers now break into corporate networks in an average of 29 minutes, and as fast as 27 seconds. Elsewhere, unpatched flaws forced emergency shutdowns of European and Japanese government websites (The Chopping Block) and exposed millions of WordPress sites (Unchained).
    What's next
    • OpenAI and Anthropic's safety rules for security researchers: if they keep blocking defensive testing prompts, banks and security teams could abandon commercial chatbots for free open-source alternatives.
    • Software release schedules at major cloud companies over the next two quarters: if product updates slow down, Knecht's warning that security is eating half of engineering time is playing out.
    On: No Priors  ·  Empire  ·  Unchained  ·  The Chopping Block

What will computer chips look like in 2045?

Oct 22, 2026  ·  1h 09m  ·  Watch
Guests  ·  Chris Auth leads process development at Intel  ·  Kevan Fischer leads process integration at Intel  ·  Jack Kavalieros researcher at Intel Labs  ·  Tyler Osborn Director of Strategic Programs for Advanced Packaging at Intel  ·  Pushkar Ranade Chief Technology Officer at Intel

Even infinite capital cannot quickly scale leading-edge AI chip output because toolmakers like ASML cannot build machines fast enough. Intel Chief Technology Officer Pushkar Ranade warned that fab capacity can double over several years, but cannot match AI's tripling compute demand. Intel must gamble on costly manufacturing shifts to survive against TSMC's massive scale advantage.

Five things worth knowing
  1. 1High fab build costs double every four to five years, leaving fixed costs at 80% and making high factory utilization mandatory to survive against rival foundries.
  2. 2Lithography scanner constraints from suppliers like ASML limit industry wafer output to roughly double over several years; a tenfold supply surge is impossible.
  3. 3Intel uses backside power delivery on upcoming nodes to route electricity beneath transistors, which loosens surface wiring density enough to make expensive High-NA EUV lithography economically viable.
  4. 4Advanced packaging with hybrid copper bonding shrinks connection spacing down toward 0.45 microns, slashing energy consumption per bit to bypass thermal limits in multi-chip AI processors.
  5. 5Caveat: Intel remains roughly three times smaller in advanced foundry volume than TSMC, leaving it vulnerable to immense cost penalties if its ambitious new process nodes face customer design delays.
“One thing you learn in this industry is that you will never have a complete data set. If you want to wait for a complete data set, you've waited too long, it's already too late.”Dwarkesh Podcast  ·  25:04

ConnectsSurging AI model training faces a hard physical ceiling as fab construction timelines and specialized tool backlogs decouple hardware supply from software demand.
